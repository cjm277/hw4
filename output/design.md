# Campus Customs: Storefront Design (Problem 10)

Goal: make the site feel like walking into the Campus Customs shop on Broadway, so shoppers trust it, stay longer, and find something to buy. Screenshots are in `output/screenshots/p10-*.jpg`.

## What changed, and why it helps sell

| Change | What it looks like | Why it helps customers stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Yale palette** | Yale navy `#00356b` (structure, headings), royal blue `#2456d3` (buttons, prices, links), powder blue `#b9d4f1` (accents, chips, borders), white (cards), and off-white `#f7f5ef` (page). Stock badges stay red / orange. | Feels unmistakably Bulldog Blue without being a wall of navy. Royal blue is used **only** for things you can act on (buttons, prices, links), so the eye goes straight to "Shop", "Add", and "$58.00". |
| **Navy "Y" backdrop** | The margins behind the store show navy with a tiled pattern of white serif Yale **Y**s (an inline SVG, ~8% opacity). The store itself sits on an off-white panel with a soft shadow, like a shop window. The same pattern runs quietly behind the hero, banners, footer, and chat header. | Instant school pride and a premium, framed "storefront" feel. The pattern is faint enough never to fight the products. |
| **Campus Customs wordmark** | The top-left "CC" button is replaced with the store's real logo, rebuilt in type from the logo on campuscustoms.com and the shop sign: an oversized italic **C** shared by **AMPUS / USTOMS**, heavy squared sports lettering (Saira Black Italic, the closest web font), a navy→royal gradient with a powder outline, and **EST. 1975** underneath. A white version is in the footer. | Shoppers who know the Broadway store recognize it immediately, and "Est. 1975" signals a real, long-standing local business. Built in code, it stays crisp at any size and loads instantly. |
| **Inter everywhere** | Inter (400–900) for all text. Headlines are 800–900 weight with tight tracking. Small caps-style "eyebrow" labels in royal blue head each section. | Clean, modern, very readable on phones. A clear heading → label → body hierarchy makes pages scannable, so people find categories and prices faster. |
| **Yale photography (no people)** | **Home:** Harkness Tower in a Gothic-arch "window" (hero), a full-width **Yale Bowl** game-day band, **Sterling Memorial Library** in "Visit the shop". **Products:** Sterling Law Building towers. **About:** Yale Law School's front. **Log in / Create account:** Sterling Library beside the form. All are openly licensed on Wikimedia Commons and credited in the footer. | Ties the gear to the places students and alumni love, the emotional reason they buy Yale merch. The Yale Bowl band sells game-day tees and hoodies directly ("Shop game-day tees"). |
| **Product presentation** | Category tiles show a real product from each category with a navy fade and label. Product photos fill their cards edge to edge. On hover a card lifts, the photo zooms slightly, and a "View details →" pill appears. Prices are bold navy. Size-level stock badges sit under the price. Item pages have a framed image, a big royal price, and colour-coded size cells (orange = only a few left). | Shoppers see the actual merch before clicking. Clear hover feedback invites a click, and price plus stock are visible at a glance, which means fewer dead ends and faster decisions. |
| **Motion (purposeful, subtle)** | A scrolling ticker of real store facts (licensed gear, 57 Broadway, 30-day returns). The hero text rises in. Sections and product cards fade up as you scroll, with a short stagger. The Harkness photo slowly pans. The Yale Bowl band has gentle parallax. All motion turns off for anyone whose device asks for reduced motion. | Makes the shop feel alive and premium, rewards scrolling, and guides attention down the page. Nothing moves during typing or checkout-type moments, and accessibility is respected. |
| **Chat feel** | The chat launcher is a royal-to-navy pill with a chat icon that pulses three times on load. The panel pops up from the corner. The header has the navy gradient, the Y pattern, and a "C" monogram avatar. Shopper messages are royal-blue bubbles and assistant messages are white with a powder edge. Product cards inside chat keep their stock badges. There's a round send button. | Feels like a branded shop assistant, not a generic widget, so shoppers are more likely to ask and get guided to a product. The pulse draws first-time visitors to the chat without nagging. |
| **Small trust details** | "Officially licensed · New Haven, CT · Est. 1975" eyebrow, "Visit the shop" with the real address, family-run since 1975 on About, and photo credits. | Trust drives purchases. Every claim on the page is factual (licensed, address, returns policy, founding year). |

## Notes
- **Accessibility:** Inter at good contrast on off-white. Every photo has descriptive alt text. Keyboard focus shows a royal outline. `prefers-reduced-motion` disables animation.
- **Responsive:** on phones the Y margins shrink to a thin frame, the nav collapses to a Menu button, the hero stacks (text, then arch), and category tiles go two-up.
